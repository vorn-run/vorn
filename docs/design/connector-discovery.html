Vorn · design note

A list you can decide from

Today a connector is a name, a blurb and an Add button. That was fine for one. With a connector arriving most days, the question stops being “which of these two” and becomes “will this one do what I need” — and nothing on screen answers it until after you have installed it.

Where it stands

The card says almost nothing

Everything the list knows is in this row. A name, a sentence, a button. Whether it can only watch for things or can also be called from a step, what it will ask you for, whether you already have the credentials — all of it is on the other side of an install.

The change

Say what it does before it is downloaded

Every connector already describes itself — its triggers, its actions, the settings it reads — and the catalog is now generated from exactly that. So the list can show it. Not a longer blurb somebody wrote, but the connector’s own manifest, which cannot drift from what you get.

Why this shape

Three questions, in the order people ask them

Grid, then detail, rather than a longer row. The grid answers is it here at a glance across twenty cards; the panel answers will it do what I need without leaving the page or installing anything.

  1. Is the thing I want here? Search covers name, description and the keywords a connector declares — so “boards” and “tfs” both find Azure DevOps. Category chips narrow a wall to a shelf.
  2. Will it do what I need? Triggers and actions, verbatim from the manifest. “1 trigger, 1 action” on the card is the count; the panel is the list, with each one’s own description.
  3. What will it cost me to set up? The settings it will ask for, optional ones visibly optional, and one line on how it authenticates. Nobody should discover a required API key on step three of a form.

The Installed filter and the Connected tag come from what is already configured, so the same view answers “what do I have” — which is the other half of why people open this page.

Where the words come from

Nothing on this screen is written twice

Every string in that panel is generated from the connector’s own manifest, upstream, and checked in CI. Rename a trigger and the list renames it. That is the difference between a catalog and a brochure: the brochure keeps advertising last month’s feature.

Two things it deliberately does not do. It does not run anything to populate the list — twenty connectors would mean twenty npx launches to open a page. And it does not trust any of this once you click Add: the connection form still probes the package you actually installed, so what you configure always matches what you got.

Build order

Three steps, each shippable

  1. Grid and search. Replace the row list with cards, trigger and action counts, category chips, keyword search. Useful on its own the moment there are more than about six connectors.
  2. The detail panel. Triggers, actions, settings, auth line, npm link. This is where the manifest data earns its place.
  3. Freshness. A quiet “updated 2 hours ago · check now” under the search box, calling the refresh the catalog already exposes. Only worth building once connectors start arriving weekly.

Step one is the whole value if connectors stay in single digits. Step two is what makes a directory of thirty navigable. Neither needs the other to land.
